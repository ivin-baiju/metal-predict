How much a website costs in Mexico and how to compare quotes

Kigo website projects start at US$1,500. Compare quotes for your business in Mexico: deliverables, currency, payments, account access and renewals.

Steven PottsOct 20, 20266 min.

If you're comparing quotes for a website in Mexico, the total is one part of the decision. You also need to know what you'll receive, what your team needs to supply and which expenses will continue after launch.

Here's a concrete reference: Kigo website projects start at US$1,500, in US dollars. That's our minimum budget. We agree on the scope and final price for each project.

You can also try our website cost calculator. The Spanish version estimates in Mexican pesos using a fixed reference of 19 MXN per US dollar; the English version shows US dollars.

I'd start by making the proposals comparable. Here's a way to review them before hiring, especially if you're receiving amounts in different currencies or packages with different inclusions.

Send the same request to each provider

Prepare a short description of the work and use it with everyone. Include what your business does, which pages you need, what visitors should be able to do and which tools you already use.

Add the available content, languages and your preferred date. Identify who can review progress. If something still needs a decision, list it as an open question so each provider explains what they're assuming in their quote.

For example: “Our services are described in a presentation. We need to know whether your proposal includes turning it into website copy.” That lets you compare responsibility for the same task.

If you first want to understand what you're hiring someone to do, see the guide to professional website design costs. If you're replacing a site, the redesign cost guide helps you prepare an inventory of the existing work.

Check what explains the difference between two proposals

Imagine receiving these two quotes. This comparison uses hypothetical terms; each project needs its own proposal.

ItemProposal AProposal B
CopyThe client supplies finished textIncludes writing it based on interviews
PhotosUses the client's imagesIncludes selecting licensed images
HostingArranged separatelyIncludes the first year
HandoverAccess to edit the websiteAccess and a session to practice updates
SupportSpecifies a period for fixing faultsAlso offers an optional monthly service

Before comparing the totals, note what you'd need to arrange or prepare separately in each case. If you already have approved copy, extra writing may add little. If everything needs writing, you need to account for that work.

Ask for clarification when an item says “included” without explaining its limits. In this example, you still need to confirm the hosting renewal price and what each support period covers. That detail could change your decision.

Clarify assumptions and exclusions

Check what the provider expects to receive in order to deliver. They might need approved copy, domain access, usable photos or a decision about the booking system.

Ask the provider to explain in the proposal what happens if those materials arrive late or change after approval. Also find out how an additional feature is requested, estimated and authorized.

Read the exclusions in full. If the proposal leaves out translation, uploading articles or a CRM connection, decide who will handle that work and how they'll coordinate. That lets you budget for the whole project.

Agree on how you'll check the finished work

Define what you'll review before considering the job complete. “Five-page website” leaves questions about content, forms and editing.

For a form, you could agree on a phone test: complete the steps, receive confirmation and check that the enquiry reached its intended destination. For the content manager, you could request a demonstration of updating a service.

On the Grupo Domo website, the quotation guides visitors through the shipment route, goods and contact details. That journey illustrates a deliverable you can describe and review step by step. The case shows the work built; your project's scope belongs in its own quote.

Write down who reviews, how they collect feedback and which corrections are included before accepting the work.

Confirm whether you'll pay in MXN or USD

A $ symbol on its own leaves a question that's easy to resolve. Ask for the currency beside each amount and for the proposal to show the total, the taxes accounted for and any separate charges.

If the quote is in dollars and you'll pay in pesos, agree on how each payment amount will be determined. Ask which reference and date will be used for conversion, and who covers possible payment fees. Put those terms in writing.

Also review invoicing requirements and when you'll receive the agreed payment documentation. Your comparison should use the terms each provider confirms for your purchase.

Connect payments to clear milestones

Review what triggers each payment and what you'll be able to assess at that point. A proposal might include a deposit and payments tied to design approval, a preview or publication. Ask for those stages to be described.

Clear payment dates also help your team organize reviews. Confirm what happens if an approval is delayed and how outstanding feedback will be resolved.

If work is added, ask for an updated cost and schedule before authorizing it. Keep the version of the proposal that both sides have accepted.

Establish who controls accounts and renewals

Request a list of the necessary accounts: domain, hosting, content management system and connected services. For each one, confirm who will own it, what access your business will have and who receives renewal notices.

Ask how access can be recovered if the responsible person changes. Also clarify which files and documentation you'll receive, and which licenses have their own usage or renewal terms.

Separate one-time expenses from monthly and annual charges. Check the price after any included period, who pays each provider and what happens if you end the maintenance service. Then you can organize renewals without having to remember every date.

Bring your questions into the final proposal

Before accepting, collect the answers in an updated quote. Check that it reflects the agreed scope, payments, responsibilities and handover.
